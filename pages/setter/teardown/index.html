BoringSales, taken apart.

A teardown of boringsales.ai from its public pages (home, guarantee, services, terms, privacy, data deletion, sitemap, robots, page source), and exactly where Witchcraft Setter beats it.

Bottom line

  1. What it is: a three-month-old, done-for-you AI voice setter for inbound leads. It calls within 60 seconds, qualifies, books, and follows up by SMS. It is sold to US home-service SMBs, and the same team also runs their Meta ads.
  2. Strongest asset: positioning. "You don't have a lead problem, you have a speed-to-revenue problem" is sharp, true, and backed by an inbound-only, consent-first legal setup.
  3. Biggest gap: it stops at "booked". There is no live hand-off to a closer, no show-rate engine, one channel plus SMS, and a human listens to every call, which caps how far it can scale.
  4. Proof gap: there are zero product results on the site. The "12–15 deals per 100 leads" figure is labelled a goal, and every testimonial is about the founder's consulting, not the product.
  5. How we win: first touch in 10 seconds on 7 channels, live transfer of hot leads, no-show rescue, AI scoring of 100% of calls, pay per showed call, and a public scoreboard. High-ticket teams first.

Offer & positioning

"We install a proven appointment setter in your business in under an hour. It calls every lead, instantly qualifies them, and books the appointment ready for you to close. If it doesn't perform, you don't pay."
  • Category they claim: "The only team that specializes in speed to revenue". Their single metric is the lead-to-deal ratio.
  • Enemy framing: email and SMS autoresponders ("avoidance dressed up as action"), blaming the lead source, the "magic-button AI fantasy" (a swipe at 11x-style AI SDRs), and the bloated $85K tech stack.
  • Differentiator claimed: "Built by closers, not coders". Closers write the scripts and rebuttals; "an expert seller … listens to every call, scores it, and coaches the AI".
  • Who it is for: "home-service or SMB business (HVAC, roofing, plumbing)". The services page adds coaches and local service providers.
  • Integrations: Salesforce, HubSpot, GoHighLevel, Calendly, Google Calendar, Zapier.
  • Channels: phone call with a "natural AI voice", then "follows up by text". No DMs, WhatsApp, email or video.
  • Second product: they run the client's Meta ads inside the client's own ad account and read the results back. Ads plus setter are sold as one system.

Pricing & the guarantee, read closely

Pricing is not published. The terms describe it as a one-time setup fee + recurring subscription (monthly or annual) + usage-based charges (e.g. call volume or minutes), with trials "from time to time".

The guarantee is conditional, and it pays out on activity, not outcomes. "Perform" means the setter "calls your inbound leads fast, qualifies them, and books appointments". To qualify you need:

  • at least 50 fresh, opted-in inbound leads a month, each no more than 6 months old, with consent to call;
  • a connected calendar with reasonable availability, and no pausing, throttling or diverting of lead flow;
  • to attend the appointments it books, complete onboarding, and route all eligible leads to them rather than competing channels.

The remedy is to "keep working at no additional cost", and only then refund subscription fees for the period. It excludes low volume, offer or price changes, and "unworked appointments". Liability is capped at the greater of 3 months' fees or US$100. Their weak spot: a setter that books no-shows still "performed".

  • Testimonials: six, all about "working with Malik" (strategy, ads, clarity). The page itself says they are from "business owners who have worked directly with the closers now building BoringSales". None describe the product.

Legal posture

This part is well drafted, and we should match it. The client warrants TCPA, state "mini-TCPA" and two-party recording consent, and promises to honour do-not-call lists, opt-outs and calling-time limits. The terms also include an "Individual Results Vary" section, a warranty disclaimer, indemnification by the client, and binding arbitration with a class-action waiver. Their data-deletion page exists for Meta app review, because they hold Facebook Page and ad-account tokens.

Strengths vs gaps

What they do well

  • Crisp "speed-to-revenue" message, one metric
  • Inbound-only, consent-first, legally clean
  • Closer-written scripts plus human QC: good early quality
  • Ads plus setter as one system, so they control both halves
  • Solid tracking (Pixel + CAPI) and their own CRM
  • Answer-engine-ready site

Where they are exposed

  • Voice and SMS only. No DM, WhatsApp, email or video
  • No live transfer: every hot lead waits for a booked slot
  • Nothing on show rate or no-shows
  • A human listens to every call, so QC cost grows with volume
  • Guarantee is on activity; pricing hidden
  • No product results. Brand rests on one founder
  • US-only demo. Low-ticket home services first

What the matrix says

  • The white space is real. No one found combines all three of live transfer to a closer, explicit no-show handling, and billing per showed call. Regal.ai and Synthflow do the first two, but only for enterprise, on custom quotes.
  • DM setters are cheap and crowded ($25–$1,000 a month): Setor, SetSmart, Clinchd, Appointwise. They offer human takeover but no voice and nothing on shows. Our price has to be justified by showed calls, not by message volume.
  • Nobody prices on outcome. The closest are per-call (Smith.ai, about $1.67–$2.50) and per-message (CloseBot, $0.012). Pay per showed call is ours to own.
  • Build, don't compete, with voice APIs. Bland, Retell and Vapi ($0.05–$0.31 a minute) are the voice layer to build on, not rivals.
  • Closest threats: CloseBot (cheap, already sells into agencies and coaching through GoHighLevel), SetSmart (same high-ticket coach positioning), and Regal.ai if it launches self-serve.
